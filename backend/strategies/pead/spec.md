**Category:** equity (long/short)
**Target Sharpe (OOS 2023-2024):** 0.50
**Author:** AlphaDesk quant — Phase 1 Wave D
**Date:** 2026-04-17

---

## 1. Motivation

Post-Earnings Announcement Drift is the oldest and most-replicated anomaly in
the empirical asset-pricing literature. First documented by Ball & Brown
(1968), formalised by Bernard & Thomas (1989, 1990), and refined by Livnat &
Mendenhall (2006) and Chordia-Goyal-Sadka-Shivakumar (2009), PEAD says that
prices react incompletely to quarterly earnings announcements: names with
large positive surprises drift upward for 30-60 trading days after the
announcement, and names with large negative surprises drift downward.

The premise is that retail and slower institutional capital underreact to
information in the earnings release. The half-life of the drift has shortened
meaningfully since 2005 as institutional arb capital has grown; modern
post-cost Sharpe on a long/short PEAD book runs in the 0.3-0.7 range for the
2019-2024 US market, well below the 1.0-1.5 of the 1980s-1990s literature.

The audit (`audit-reports/strategy-02-pead.md`) scored the legacy
AlphaDesk PEAD implementation at **18/100**. The defining defects were:

1. **No SUE, no consensus, no σ** — the "surprise" was `|change_pct|`
   vs a static sector-average price reaction dict, not `(actual − estimate)
   / σ_surprise`.
2. **Hardcoded weekday earnings calendar** — the "calendar" was a 5-key
   dict keyed on `date.today().weekday()`.
3. **Demo-RNG screener** — the universe of candidates was 14 hardcoded
   tickers with metrics generated by `random.Random(hash(sym))`.
4. **Short leg silently dropped** — the strategy's `analyze()` emitted
   `signal=sell` for negative surprises but the runner filter
   `if sig != "buy": continue` discarded them.
5. **No time-based exit** — the 40-day drift window constant existed
   but was never invoked; positions lived or died on a 4% hard stop and
   an 8-14% take-profit, which inverts PEAD's positive-skew payoff.

This rewrite addresses every defect explicitly. Data comes from real FMP
endpoints (earnings calendar + historical surprises), SUE is computed
from the trailing 8-quarter standard deviation of the forecast error per
Bernard-Thomas, and the 40-day drift window with MOC time-stop exit is
the only exit mechanism — no hard stops on the drift signal.

  Income Numbers." *Journal of Accounting Research* 6(2): 159-178. The

  27: 1-36. Defines the seasonal-random-walk-based SUE and the 60-day
  canonical drift window.
- **Bernard, V. & Thomas, J. (1990).** "Evidence that Stock Prices Do Not
  Fully Reflect the Implications of Current Earnings for Future Earnings."
  *Journal of Accounting and Economics* 13: 305-340. Shows the autocorrelation
  structure of the drift across subsequent quarters.
- **Livnat, J. & Mendenhall, R. (2006).** "Comparing the Post-Earnings
  Announcement Drift for Surprises Calculated from Analyst and Time Series
  Forecasts." *Journal of Accounting Research* 44(1): 177-205. Establishes
  that I/B/E/S-analyst-consensus SUE dominates the SRW variant for
  post-1990 data.
- **Chordia, T., Goyal, A., Sadka, G., Sadka, R. & Shivakumar, L. (2009).**
  "Liquidity and the Post-Earnings-Announcement Drift." *Financial Analysts

  and that the drift shortens to ~10-15 trading days once liquidity

## 3. Signal math

### 3.1 Inputs

- Daily OHLCV bars from `ctx.bar_provider` (Alpaca, split/dividend-adjusted).
- Earnings calendar from `ctx.earnings_provider.calendar(start, end)`:
  columns `[symbol, date, eps_actual, eps_estimated,
  revenue_actual, revenue_estimated, last_updated]`.
- Historical surprises from `ctx.earnings_provider.surprises(symbol,
  start, end)`: columns `[symbol, date, eps_actual, eps_estimated,
  surprise, surprise_pct, sue, revenue_actual, revenue_estimated]`. The
  trailing surprise standard deviation used for the SUE denominator is
  computed from this frame.

### 3.2 SUE (Standardized Unexpected Earnings)

For a name `s` reporting on day `D`:

```
SUE(s, D) = (eps_actual(s, D) - eps_estimated(s, D)) / σ_surprise(s, D)
```

where `σ_surprise(s, D)` is the sample standard deviation (ddof=1) of the
last `sue_lookback_quarters` historical surprises for `s` strictly before
`D`. Default `sue_lookback_quarters = 8` (two years of quarters, per
Bernard-Thomas canonical spec). We require at least 4 historical quarters;
names without enough history are skipped.

SUE is unitless — a z-score on the surprise magnitude. It is the standard
measure in the PEAD literature precisely because it normalises across
firms with different expected-surprise variances.

### 3.3 Signal generation (each trading day `D`)

At the start of day `D`:

1. Fetch the earnings calendar for day `D-1` (we treat names reporting on
   `D-1` as having information incorporated by the `D-1` close; their
   first clean post-announcement trading session is `D`). This captures
   both BMO reporters (reporting morning of `D-1`) and AMC reporters
   (reporting after the close of `D-1`) — in both cases the market has
   absorbed the news by the `D-1` close.
2. For each name `s` with an announcement on `D-1`:
   - Compute SUE using the `eps_actual` / `eps_estimated` from the
     calendar row and the trailing-quarter σ from the surprises endpoint.
   - Apply the **universe filter**: `market_cap > min_mcap_bn` (proxy via
     a static large-cap list — FMP point-in-time market cap is $0.01/call
     and the 5B floor is a coarse filter), `dollar ADV > 20M` (computed
     from daily bars), `close > 10`.
   - Apply the **overlapping-earnings filter**: skip the name if it has
     *another* earnings announcement scheduled in the next
     `holding_days` days. This avoids holding a position into a fresh
     announcement, which has a 10x-higher next-day variance than a
     typical session.
3. Rank the surviving names by `|SUE|` descending. Take the top
   `sue_universe_rank_top_pct` of the day's surprises (or `1.0` to keep
   all). This is a soft-ranking filter on days with many small surprises.
4. For each remaining name:
   - If `SUE > sue_threshold`: emit a long MOO entry for day `D`'s open.
   - If `SUE < -sue_threshold` and `allow_shorts`: emit a short MOO entry.
   - Otherwise skip.
5. Stage the entry signal with `target_weight = ±allocation_per_position`.
   Position count is capped at `max_concurrent_positions`; if capacity
   is exhausted we skip the weakest-|SUE| candidates.

### 3.4 Management / exit (each trading day)

For each open PEAD position with entry date `D_entry`:

- If `D - D_entry >= holding_days` (in trading days): emit a
  `target_weight = 0` MOC exit to close at today's close.

Canonical `holding_days = 40` per Bernard-Thomas 1989; alternatives
{20, 30, 60} are tuned. **No hard stop-loss, no take-profit.** PEAD is
a positive-skew, time-exit payoff — bracket orders clip the distribution
and destroy the signal.

### 3.5 Sizing

Fixed-fraction equal-weight. Each position gets
`allocation_per_position` of initial equity (default 5%). The short
leg is sized symmetrically. Borrow cost on shorts is handled by the
engine's `Portfolio.accrue_borrow_cost` at the default 1%/yr rate;
we do not implement an HTB lookup in Phase 1 (deferred to Phase 2).

## 4. Universe

PEAD has its strongest edge on mid-caps and small-caps (Chordia et al.
2009 show the effect is concentrated in low-liquidity names), but a
production-tradable version on a retail broker needs liquidity. We
compromise on a broad S&P 500 universe with a liquidity floor:

- Seed: 200 hand-curated S&P 500 names spanning all 11 GICS sectors.
  Hand-curation (vs dynamic constituent lookup) is a Phase 1 simplification;
  S&P 500 membership is stable enough over 2019-2024 that a fixed list
  captures >95% of the tradable opportunity.
- Daily filter:
  - `dollar ADV > 20M` over trailing 90 days (Chordia liquidity cut).
  - `close > 10` (price floor; small-cap PEAD doesn't survive retail
    spreads below $10).
  - `market_cap > 5B` (proxy — the curated seed list is all mid/large).
  - No overlapping earnings in the next `holding_days` days.

## 5. Parameters and search space

Defaults below; Optuna ranges in `config.py`.

| Param | Default | Range | Notes |
|---|---:|---|---|
| `sue_threshold` | 1.5 | [1.0, 3.0] | |SUE| gate for entry |
| `holding_days` | 40 | {20, 30, 40, 60} | Bernard-Thomas canonical |
| `sue_lookback_quarters` | 8 | {4, 8, 12} | σ denominator window |
| `max_concurrent_positions` | 10 | {5, 10, 15, 20} | book concentration |
| `allocation_per_position` | 0.05 | [0.03, 0.10] | per-name fraction |
| `allow_shorts` | True | {True, False} | disable for long-only regimes |
| `universe_min_mcap_bn` | 5 | {2, 5, 10} | liquidity floor (proxy) |
| `sue_universe_rank_top_pct` | 1.0 | [0.05, 0.20] | top-decile daily filter |

Fixed (not searched): `adv_usd_min = 20e6`, `price_min = 10.0`.

## 6. Known failure modes

- **Q2 2020 COVID disruption.** Almost every company missed or withdrew
  guidance; SUE distribution is wildly bimodal. The trailing-8Q σ is
  inflated post-2020Q2, which suppresses |SUE| and *reduces* signal
  in 2020Q3-2021Q1. Documented in the OOS report.
- **Biotech binary events.** Small pharma names with catalyst releases
  (FDA approvals) can have 5σ EPS surprises because the denominator
  σ is tiny on a company with one line-item of R&D spend. The 5B
  market-cap floor removes most of these.
- **IPO recency.** A company with <4 quarters of history has no σ to
  compute SUE against. These are silently dropped.
- **Earnings revisions.** FMP's historical surprise data reflects
  eventually-revised EPS figures. We accept this as a realistic
  approximation; strict point-in-time handling would require an
  I/B/E/S snapshot store (deferred to Phase 2).

## 8. Deviations from textbook Bernard-Thomas

**Kept:**
- SUE = `(actual − estimated) / σ_surprise`, denominator = trailing 8Q
  standard deviation.
- 40-day drift window as the default holding period.
- Long + short deciles (both directions traded when `allow_shorts=True`).
- Entry at next open after the announcement (T+1 open).

**Modified:**
- **Analyst consensus, not seasonal random walk.** FMP `epsEstimated`
  is the analyst consensus at time-of-release; Bernard-Thomas 1989
  used `EPS_{t-4}` because analyst data was unavailable in the
  pre-I/B/E/S era. Livnat-Mendenhall 2006 showed the analyst variant
  dominates post-1990, and we follow them.
- **Fixed fraction sizing vs decile-weighted.** Bernard-Thomas formed
  deciles daily; we use a simpler capped-concurrent-positions model.
  On a compact universe and a 40-day holding period, the decile
  structure and the "take top K by |SUE|" structure are
  near-equivalent and the latter is easier to reason about.
- **Liquidity floor.** Large-cap only. Pure academic PEAD is stronger
  on small-caps; we accept the lower edge in exchange for tradable
  execution.

**Dropped:**
- **Options overlay** (the dead textbook file had bull/bear verticals).
  Vertical debit spreads eat the drift with theta; the instrument is
  wrong. Equity only.
- **Revenue-beat filter** — the audit's "revenue beat confirmation"
  was actually the Piotroski F-score, a misnomer. We use the pure
  SUE signal; adding revenue-SUE is a Phase 2 refinement.
- **Guidance confirmation.** FMP does not carry an API-clean guidance
  signal; the textbook file's `+0.2 / -0.1` bonus was ad-hoc. Dropped.
- **Dollar-SUE / revenue-SUE variants.** Single-EPS-SUE captures the
  canonical effect; multi-signal combinations overfit on a 2-year
  OOS window.

## 9. Test coverage

`tests/test_strategy.py` exercises:

1. **SUE computation** from a synthetic surprise history (8 quarters,
   trailing σ known, expected SUE computed by hand).
2. **Directional gating** — positive SUE → long signal, negative SUE →
   short signal (only when `allow_shorts=True`), small |SUE| → no
   signal.
3. **40-day time-stop exit** — a position opened on day D_entry is
   exited on D_entry + holding_days via MOC.
4. **Overlapping-earnings filter** — a name with an earnings
   announcement scheduled mid-holding is skipped.
5. **FMP endpoint smoke** — a fake `EarningsProvider` returning a
   hand-built calendar + surprise frame produces the expected signals
   end-to-end.

All tests run in <1s with no network.
