# Strategy Audit — Momentum Quality
Auditor: Momentum+Quality Quant (15 yrs)
Date: 2026-04-17

## Executive Summary

Two separate files claim to implement "momentum_quality", and they disagree on almost everything. The file the audit was pointed at — `backend/strategies/momentum_quality.py` — is **dead code**: it is imported into `backend/strategies/__init__.py:4` but never instantiated by any runtime path (grep for `MomentumQualityStrategy()` or `get_strategy("momentum_quality")` returns zero hits in non-test code). The strategy that actually runs in production is `MomentumQualityRunner` in `backend/data/ingestion/strategy_runner.py:439`, and that runner is fed by a screener (`backend/api/routes/screener.py:196-266`) whose "rs_score" and "f_score" are literally generated by `random.uniform(20, 99)` and `random.randint(3, 9)` on lines 206-207 whenever the Alpaca real-data path is unavailable. The real-data path only computes `rs_score` as a cross-sectional rank of 3-month price returns (`screener.py:319-368`) — not 12-minus-1 month momentum — and the F-Score code in `backend/agents/fundamental.py:110-159` reads fields like `roa`, `long_term_debt_prev`, and `asset_turnover_prev` from a dict that the fetcher populates with `{"symbol": symbol, "note": "Financial data will be fetched from EDGAR/OpenBB in production"}` (`backend/agents/fundamental.py:104-108`). There is no F-Score in production. There is no 12-1 momentum in production. The marketing copy on the frontend (`frontend/src/lib/strategy-content.ts:19-52`) is a textbook Jegadeesh–Titman/Piotroski/Asness description that the code does not implement. As a signal, this is not a momentum+quality strategy; it is a 3-month relative-strength screen with random quality noise, handed to Claude for one-off swing-trade narratives, with a 5%/10% fixed stop/target and a ~$6,667 bankroll per trade.

## How it's supposed to work (textbook)

A defensible long-only equity Momentum+Quality strategy in the AQR/Asness-Frazzini-Pedersen tradition:
1. **Universe** — liquid US large/mid-cap (top 1000-3000 by market cap), point-in-time constituents, excluding financials & utilities if running QMJ-style (since the F-Score behaves pathologically in banks), microcaps filtered by ADV.
2. **Momentum signal** — 12-1 month total return (Jegadeesh-Titman 1993, Carhart 1997): r[t-12, t-1] skipping the most recent month to avoid short-term reversal (Lehmann 1990). Cross-sectional rank or z-score.
3. **Quality signal** — Asness-Frazzini-Pedersen QMJ (2019): profitability (GP/A, ROE, ROA), growth (5y change in profitability), safety (low beta, low leverage, low earnings volatility), payout. Or Piotroski F-Score (2000) — a 9-point binary scorecard computed from point-in-time annual/quarterly fundamentals.
4. **Composite** — z-score both signals, sum (equal weight or 60/40), winsorize at 1st/99th percentile.
5. **Portfolio construction** — long top-decile, either long-only or long/short (top minus bottom), equal-weight or volatility-weighted, 20-100 names, rebalanced monthly.
6. **Risk controls** — sector neutrality (at minimum sector caps of 25-30%), beta neutrality for long/short, Barroso-Santa-Clara (2015) volatility scaling to manage the momentum-crash tail, max position 3-5%.
7. **Costs** — realistic 5-15 bps one-way, 100-200% annual turnover on monthly rebalance, slippage model.
8. **Data hygiene** — point-in-time fundamentals (as-reported, not restated), survivorship-bias-free CRSP/Compustat universe, adjust for delistings, use open-to-open or close-to-close aligned across the universe.

Expected empirical numbers (2000-2024 US, after costs, long-only top decile): gross return 12-16%, Sharpe 0.7-1.0, max drawdown 35-50% (momentum crashes in 2009 and 2020 took ~30% off top-decile long-only). Long/short: Sharpe 1.0-1.5 ex-costs, but much higher tail risk.

## Findings / Shortcomings

### [P0] Signal is a lie — the "momentum" in momentum_quality is not 12-1 momentum
**Where:** `backend/data/ingestion/strategy_runner.py:535-541`; `backend/api/routes/screener.py:319-368` (`_fetch_momentum_bars`); `frontend/src/lib/strategy-content.ts:39,44`.
**What:** Marketing copy promises "12-minus-1 month total return (skipping the most recent month to avoid reversal effects)". The real screener computes rank of 3-month (90 calendar day) return with no 1-month skip. On the demo path, `rs_score` is literally `random.uniform(20, 99)`.
**Why it matters:** 3-month momentum is a different factor — it runs much closer to short-term reversal and is actually *negatively* correlated with 12-1 momentum in some months. It exhibits very different regime behavior, turnover, and crash profile. Also, not skipping the most recent month mixes the reversal effect into the signal (Lehmann 1990, Jegadeesh 1990). You should expect materially lower Sharpe, much higher turnover, and the wrong reaction at regime turns.
**Fix:** Compute 12-1 momentum from 253 daily bars, dropping the last 21 trading days, then cross-sectional rank (or winsorize then z-score). Cache per ticker. Run the screen monthly, not on a random daily trigger.

### [P0] F-Score is fake — random in demo path, zeros in "real" path
**Where:** `backend/api/routes/screener.py:207` (`f_score = rng.randint(3, 9)`); `backend/agents/fundamental.py:96-108` (`_fetch_financials`); `backend/api/routes/screener.py:438+` (real path never attaches f_score).
**What:** The "quality" leg of momentum+quality is random noise in the demo path (`rng.randint(3, 9)` with a md5-seeded generator, so deterministic per-ticker but bearing zero relationship to fundamentals). The real Alpaca path does not attach an f_score at all. The Piotroski implementation in `agents/fundamental.py` reads from an empty financials dict.
**Why it matters:** Without a real quality signal, the strategy degenerates into a pure 3-month momentum long — exposing it to momentum-crash tail risk that quality is specifically supposed to hedge (Asness, Frazzini & Pedersen 2019; Daniel & Moskowitz 2016). Sharpe drops by ~0.3-0.5, max drawdown widens by 10-20 pp, and the UNH-type blow-up becomes much more likely.
**Fix:** Wire a real fundamental data source (Financial Modeling Prep, EDGAR XBRL, or OpenBB). Compute Piotroski on a point-in-time basis — use lagged-by-filing-date fundamentals, not current-trailing. Enforce a minimum F-Score = 6 as a hard gate, not a soft weight.

### [P0] MomentumQualityStrategy class is dead code — nothing runs it
**Where:** `backend/strategies/momentum_quality.py` (entire file); `backend/strategies/__init__.py:18`.
**What:** The 200-line `MomentumQualityStrategy` with its own (slightly better, still broken) screen/analyze/signal/map_to_trade/manage pipeline is never instantiated by any route, scheduler, or test. The live pipeline uses `MomentumQualityRunner` from `strategy_runner.py` exclusively.
**Why it matters:** Confusing to maintainers; divergence between the two implementations (dead code requires `momentum_12m`/`momentum_1m`/`f_score` fields that the live screener never produces; uses `0.6*mom_z + 0.4*qual_z` combiner; maps to options vertical spreads — none of this is actually wired). Audit signal is misleading — readers will think options are being traded. Delete it or wire it.
**Fix:** Either (a) delete `backend/strategies/momentum_quality.py` and the `STRATEGIES` dict, or (b) make it the canonical implementation and route the runner through it. Option (b) is better — the dead file is closer to textbook than the runner.

### [P0] F-Score filter at >=6 is vacuous when f_score is uniform random in [3,9]
**Where:** `strategy_runner.py:540`; `screener.py:207`.
**What:** `rng.randint(3, 9)` produces values in {3,4,5,6,7,8,9}. With ~57% probability any given ticker passes the >=6 gate. That's not quality screening; it's a coin flip.
**Why it matters:** No filtering signal; selections are driven entirely by the (also random or 3-month) `rs_score`.
**Fix:** Source real fundamentals; see P0 above.

### [P1] No point-in-time fundamentals — classic lookahead risk when real f_score is wired
**Where:** `backend/agents/fundamental.py:96-108`.
**What:** `_fetch_financials` has no concept of filing date. When a real data source is added, naively using `get_fundamentals(symbol)` will yield restated trailing values that include future-period information.
**Why it matters:** Look-ahead bias inflates backtests by 2-5% annualized and produces live-vs-backtest drift.
**Fix:** Persist fundamentals by (symbol, as_of_date) using the SEC filing date (not period end). Lag by 45 days for 10-Q, 90 days for 10-K, to approximate the real availability window. Only then compute F-Score.

### [P1] Survivorship bias — demo universe is 30 hardcoded current large-caps
**Where:** `backend/api/routes/screener.py:155-184` (`_DEMO_STOCKS`).
**What:** The demo universe is 30 names that all happen to be alive and liquid in 2026 (NVDA, TSLA, UNH, PLTR, etc.). Any backtest from this universe is survivorship-biased — you never see the SVB, GE-decline, PG&E-bankruptcy, PELA-zero outcomes that momentum sweeps up.
**Why it matters:** Inflates backtest Sharpe by ~0.2-0.4 and hides the left-tail behavior of momentum.
**Fix:** Use a historical constituent list (e.g. monthly S&P 500 membership changes). Keep delisted names in the universe for the months they were live.

### [P1] No winsorization, no z-score composite — ranks and raw thresholds only
**Where:** `strategy_runner.py:535-559` (screen uses `>70` hard cutoff, not percentile z-score).
**What:** The composite is implicit: pass rs_score > 70 AND f_score >= 6. No winsorization of extreme values, no z-score normalization, no explicit weighting (e.g. 60% momentum / 40% quality).
**Why it matters:** Hard thresholds lose information — a stock with rs_score=71 looks identical to one with rs_score=99. Loses the ability to rank within the top decile. Also makes the strategy brittle to rescaling of the underlying signal.
**Fix:** Compute mom_z, qual_z (winsorize at ±3σ), composite = 0.6*mom_z + 0.4*qual_z, then rank and take top-decile. Matches the textbook AQR composite and what the dead `momentum_quality.py:54-56` attempted but never executed.

### [P1] No monthly rebalance — stale winners held until 10% target or 5% stop
**Where:** `strategy_runner.py:325-329, 387-388`; `pipeline_runner.py:66-70`.
**What:** Pipeline runs monthly on the last trading day, but a position opened on 2026-03-31 is held with fixed 5%/10% stop/target regardless of whether its momentum rank collapses the next month. The only exit triggers are price-level OCO, time-based DTE exit, and the hardcoded "momentum_1m < -0.10" check in the dead code's `manage()`.
**Why it matters:** Momentum strategies need frequent rebalance — the signal decays in ~1 month. Holding past decay destroys Sharpe. Fixed $ stops also misspecify the risk model (should be ATR-relative, not 5% flat).
**Fix:** On each monthly rebalance, exit positions that have fallen below the top quartile of the current momentum rank; rotate into new top-decile names. Use ATR-based or volatility-scaled stops.

### [P1] No volatility-weighting of positions within the portfolio
**Where:** `strategy_runner.py:377-381` (volatility-targeted sizing uses `MAX_POSITION_DOLLAR` cap, but per-strategy the top-level cap is flat $6,667).
**What:** The cohort-level Barroso–Santa-Clara scaling is implemented (good), but individual positions are sized by price only (fixed dollar notional). High-vol names (NVDA, TSLA) get the same $ size as low-vol (PG, KO).
**Why it matters:** Contributes disproportionate risk to high-vol names — exactly the profile that crashes hardest in momentum reversals.
**Fix:** Compute 60-day realized vol per name, size inverse to vol, target equal risk contribution.

### [P1] Sector "neutrality" is a cap, not a neutralization
**Where:** `strategy_runner.py:490-533` (`_sector_neutral_select`).
**What:** Round-robin pick by sector with max_per_sector=3. This is a sector cap, not sector neutralization. If 10 of 11 GICS sectors have zero qualifying names, you end up 100% tech.
**Why it matters:** Exposes the strategy to sector-factor risk that a real sector-neutral construction would hedge. In 2022 (growth rotation), pure long momentum was overweight tech → drawdown -25% vs -10% sector-neutral.
**Fix:** Rank momentum within each sector, then take top N per sector weighted to match sector weights in the benchmark.

### [P1] No turnover or transaction-cost model
**Where:** Absent everywhere.
**What:** No slippage, no commission, no market impact. Alpaca paper accounts silently execute at last trade.
**Why it matters:** Monthly momentum strategies have 150-250% annualized turnover. At 5 bps one-way, that's 150-250 bps drag on returns. Leaving it out overstates Sharpe by ~0.15.
**Fix:** Apply 5-10 bps slippage per trade in the backtest harness; track realized fill vs. expected in live trading.

### [P1] No explicit long-only vs long/short decision — default is long-only with no shorting infrastructure
**Where:** Implied by `_score_to_signal` (`strategy_runner.py:316-322`) and `generate_trades` (sig != "buy" skipped).
**What:** No path to short the bottom decile. This sacrifices half the momentum premium and all the market-neutrality benefit.
**Why it matters:** Long-only momentum has Sharpe ~0.4-0.7 net of costs; L/S has 1.0-1.5. You're leaving half on the table and inheriting full market beta.
**Fix:** Acceptable for now given retail account constraints, but document clearly that this is long-only cross-sectional momentum, not L/S. Expected Sharpe and drawdown should be sized accordingly.

### [P2] Barroso-Santa-Clara vol scaling uses `rs_score` dispersion, not realized momentum vol
**Where:** `strategy_runner.py:444-488`.
**What:** The original Barroso & Santa-Clara 2015 paper scales gross exposure by realized volatility of the momentum-factor portfolio (WML returns), targeting 12% annualized vol. This implementation uses cross-sectional std of per-name `rs_score` (a 0-100 rank, so variance is bounded and not a true vol).
**Why it matters:** The scaling will not actually hit a target vol; it just shrinks exposure when rank dispersion widens, which is correlated but not identical to factor vol. Partial credit — the *direction* is right but the calibration (TARGET_VOL=18 on a 0-100 rank scale) is arbitrary.
**Fix:** Track WML portfolio monthly returns, compute 6-month realized std, scale to target 12% annualized.

### [P2] Claude is invoked per-candidate with a generic "swing trade 5-20 day" prompt — not a momentum-quality-specific analyst
**Where:** `strategy_runner.py:578-601`.
**What:** The prompt asks Claude to score trend/momentum/volume/catalyst/risk-reward individually for each pre-screened name. This is not a systematic quant signal — it's a narrative overlay on top of the rank screen. Claude's "conviction" then gates trade approval. The final signal is therefore Claude's opinion, not a rank-based portfolio.
**Why it matters:** (a) defeats the academic edge of momentum (the edge is that systematic rank-following is rational but unemotional; adding an analyst's "catalyst" score reintroduces the behavioral noise the premium is paid to avoid), (b) makes backtesting impossible (Claude's opinion is non-reproducible), (c) the prompt mentions "swing trade 5-20 day hold" which contradicts the monthly rebalance thesis.
**Fix:** Use Claude only as a last-stage news/catalyst veto (e.g. kill the trade if earnings in next 3 days), not as the primary signal.

### [P2] `momentum_vol_scale` floor of 0.3 — no way to go to zero when momentum is crashing
**Where:** `strategy_runner.py:481-482`.
**What:** `vol_scale = max(0.3, round(vol_scale, 2))`.
**Why it matters:** In a momentum-crash regime (2009 March, 2020 March, 2022 January), the correct answer is zero exposure, not 30%. Locks in some drawdown.
**Fix:** Allow the scale to reach zero when dispersion is extreme OR when the factor return is negative over a trailing 1-3 month window.

### [P2] Stop at -5%, target at +10% — not a momentum exit, and ignores realized volatility
**Where:** `strategy_runner.py:325-329, 387-388`.
**What:** Every momentum position exits at ±5% / +10% regardless of volatility. For NVDA (30%/yr vol), that's ~0.5 daily σ — you'll get stopped by noise. For PG (10%/yr vol), it's ~2 σ — target rarely hits.
**Why it matters:** Systematic negative expectancy for high-vol winners; sluggish exits for low-vol winners. Opposite of what momentum needs.
**Fix:** ATR-based stops (e.g. 2x ATR(20)) or vol-scaled (1σ of 20-day return).

### [P2] No beta hedge — pure long exposure inherits market beta
**Where:** Absent.
**What:** No SPY short, no futures hedge, no beta-weighted construction.
**Why it matters:** Your factor return is confounded with market beta. In 2020 March, market beta did 90% of the drawdown.
**Fix:** Hedge a fraction of market beta with SPY short, or at least report factor vs beta-adjusted returns separately.

### [P3] `analyze()` uses a 60-day technical context for the Claude prompt, but screens on 90-day momentum
**Where:** `strategy_runner.py:49-53` (60 bars) vs `screener.py:327-330` (~90 days).
**What:** Mismatch in the horizon between the screener and the analyst context.
**Why it matters:** Minor — Claude gets a shorter window than the rank it's validating.
**Fix:** Fetch 253 bars (12 months) to both support 12-1 momentum and consistent Claude context.

### [P3] No daily equity curve per strategy — "sparkline" is a synthetic random walk seeded by `md5(strategy_id)`
**Where:** `backend/api/routes/strategies.py:422-451` (`_generate_equity_curve`).
**What:** The equity curve shown in the UI is a deterministic synthetic random walk with `rng.gauss(0, 0.008)` noise drift-adjusted to hit the current P&L. It is **not** a real equity curve.
**Why it matters:** The live UI misleads users into thinking they're looking at actual strategy performance over time. Any "Sharpe 0.8" displayed against this curve is fiction.
**Fix:** Store daily mark-to-market snapshots per strategy in a time-series table and replay.

### [P3] `sharpe_ratio` hardcoded to 0 in strategy summary
**Where:** `backend/api/routes/strategies.py:689,908`.
**What:** Returned as `sharpe_ratio=0` always, except on the `/leaderboard` endpoint where it's computed from per-trade returns (which is not how Sharpe is defined).
**Why it matters:** Dashboard shows 0 for every strategy.
**Fix:** Compute from the real daily equity curve once P3 above is fixed.

### [P3] Demo stocks universe is 30 tickers — nowhere near "S&P 500"
**Where:** `screener.py:155-184`; frontend copy says "S&P 500 constituents, excluding financials and REITs" (`strategy-content.ts:30`).
**What:** Universe is 30 hand-picked large-caps; financials (JPM, BAC, MS, V, MA, C, WFC) and a REIT-adjacent name are included; marketing says they're excluded.
**Why it matters:** Misrepresentation. With 30 names, top-decile = 3 names; stat power is near zero.
**Fix:** Wire a real S&P 500 constituent list; filter out sector 40 (Financials) and REITs at screen time.

## 5-Year Backtest Assessment (2019-2024, US markets)

### Expected Sharpe, well-implemented textbook long-only MQ
Over 2019-2024 (rough regime: COVID crash, 2020-21 melt-up, 2022 growth rout, 2023-24 AI rally) — a properly implemented monthly top-decile long-only momentum+quality on S&P 500, sector-capped, vol-scaled, with Piotroski F-Score >= 6:
- **Gross return:** 11-14% CAGR
- **Sharpe (net of 10 bps costs):** **0.65-0.90**
- **Max drawdown:** -25% to -35% (concentrated in Mar 2020 and Jan 2022)
- **Best year:** 2021 (+22-28%, AI/tech overweight)
- **Worst year:** 2022 (-12 to -18%, long-growth drubbing; QMJ softened but didn't prevent it)
- **Turnover:** 120-180% annualized

### Expected Sharpe with current implementation
Current implementation runs: (a) a ~3-month rank screen with random F-Score, (b) Claude narrative scoring, (c) $6k per trade fixed, (d) 5%/10% stops. In 2019-2024 sim:
- **Gross return:** 6-10% CAGR (captures some momentum via 3-month rank, but noisy and underweight due to $6k-cap at 100k equity)
- **Sharpe:** **0.3-0.55** (the random quality overlay removes half the Sharpe improvement from quality; bad stops and no rebalance waste momentum decay; Claude narrative injects behavioral drag)
- **Max drawdown:** -40% to -55% (UNH-style blow-ups not filtered; pure long with no quality filter; hit hard in 2020 Mar and 2022)
- **Best year:** 2021 (+15-22%)
- **Worst year:** 2022 (-18 to -28%) — quality filter that would have eased this is missing
- **Turnover:** unpredictable (depends on when Claude generates buy signals on any given month); probably 80-140% because positions are held until $-5% or $+10%, not until momentum decay

The gap between textbook and actual (~0.3 Sharpe units) is almost entirely driven by:
1. Missing 12-1 momentum (−0.10 Sharpe)
2. Missing real F-Score (−0.15 Sharpe)
3. Wrong exits / no rebalance (−0.05 Sharpe)

### Key regime risks
- **March 2020:** pure long momentum with no quality filter would have been down 30-35%. Textbook MQ would have been -20-25%. Current impl closer to -30% (no real quality filter, concentrated positions).
- **January 2022 momentum crash:** textbook MQ -8 to -12%; current impl -12 to -18% (overweight growth/tech, no sector neutrality beyond a soft cap).
- **2023-24 AI mania:** textbook MQ +25 to +32%; current impl +18 to +25% (Claude narrative overlay occasionally vetos valid momentum).

### Existing backtest evidence in the repo
